About this site

The Double Exposure Explorer is an index of the people, films and sessions of the Double Exposure Investigative Film Festival in Washington, DC, from its first edition in 2015 to its twelfth in 2026. It is an independent project of the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine Labs, not a site of the festival or of 100Reporters, which runs it.

What is here

226 films, 267 forum sessions and events, and 1,319 people: filmmakers, journalists, moderators, panelists, workshop leaders, jurors and festival staff. Every person has a page listing what they did at the festival, year by year, their bios, the topics of their films and sessions, organisations named in their bio and the people they appeared with.

Where it comes from

  • The printed festival catalogues for 2015–2019 and 2022–2025, which the festival publishes as PDFs. Films, sessions, schedules, people and bios were read from them page by page; each record links to its catalogue page in the Wayback Machine. The 2024 catalogue’s text layer was broken and was read with OCR.
  • The festival website, dxfest.com: its film pages for every year, its forum session pages (2016 and 2018–2026), and its 1,222 speaker biographies.
  • For 2020 and 2021, when the festival was held online and printed no catalogue, the website is the only source. Some 2020 programme pages were never captured by the Wayback Machine.

How it was put together, and its limits

  • The catalogue text was extracted into structured records with the help of an AI model, then merged with the website’s records. Names are matched across years by spelling; variants such as “Ramona Diaz” and “Ramona S. Diaz” are joined, but two different people with the same name would be shown as one, and one person whose name was spelled very differently may appear twice.
  • Topics are assigned by keyword rules and organisations are found by name in the bios. Both are aids to discovery, not judgements.
  • Roles are as the catalogue or website gave them. Where the website lists a speaker for a session without a role, they are shown as a speaker.

Corrections are welcome: info@archive.org.

Web archives

Web archives are welcome to capture this site. If your crawler has trouble capturing it, contact info@archive.org. Every page is plain HTML with ordinary links, and there is a sitemap.

Accessibility

This site aims to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA: it has a skip link, labelled navigation and form fields, sufficient colour contrast in light and dark mode, and works with the keyboard and without JavaScript (except search and filtering). If something does not work for you, please tell us.

Who made it

This site was made by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, working in Claude Code at the direction of Mark Graham (Internet Archive). Mark Graham decided what it should be and what it should cover; Claude did the research and wrote the code.
