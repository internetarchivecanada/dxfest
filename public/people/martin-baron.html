Biography

Marty Baron became executive editor of The Washington Post on Jan. 2, 2013. He oversees The Post’s print and digital news operations.

Previously, Marty had been editor of the Boston Globe since 2001. During his tenure, the Globe won six Pulitzer Prizes — for Public Service, Explanatory Journalism, National Reporting and Criticism. The Pulitzer Prize for Public Service was awarded in 2003 for a Globe Spotlight Team investigation of the concealment of sex abuse by clergy in the Catholic Church.

Prior to the Globe, Marty held top editing positions at the New York Times, the Los Angeles Times and the Miami Herald. Under his leadership, the Miami Herald won the Pulitzer Prize for Breaking News Coverage in 2001 for its coverage of the raid to recover Elián González, a Cuban boy at the center of a fierce immigration and custody dispute.

Marty was named Editor of the Year by Editor & Publisher magazine in 2001, and Editor of the Year by the National Press Foundation in 2004.
