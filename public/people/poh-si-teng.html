Biography

Poh Si Teng is an award-winning filmmaker and the director of IDA Funds and Enterprise Program. She is the producer of St. Louis Superman, which was nominated for an Academy Award in the short documentary category, and won a Critics’ Choice Award.

Prior to joining IDA, she was the documentary commissioner overseeing the US, Canada and Latin American for Al Jazeera English’s flagship documentary strand, Witness. Poh was previously a staff journalist for The New York Times, where she was nominated for an Emmy Award for Outstanding Interview, and received both a Society of Professional Journalists’ Deadline Award and a NPPA award.

Before joining the Times, she was an independent filmmaker and journalist based in India for several years. She is one of DOC NYC’s 40 Under 40 Filmmakers to Watch. Her latest documentary, Blood On Our Side, recently won best documentary short at the Austin Film Festival.

Other bios (1)

Poh Si Teng is an Emmy-nominated director and senior commissioning producer for Witness, the flagship observational documentary strand of Al Jazeera English (International). She commissions documentaries and looks out for acquisitions and co-productions from the Americas for Witness.

She recently commissioned and senior produced Venezuela: Smuggling Dreams; Children of the FARC; Deportees Welcome: Between MS-13 and Call Centers; and A Cold War Family Reunion. (Venezuela: Smuggling Dreams is a finalist for the 2018 Rory Peck Current Affairs Award.)

Teng was previously a journalist for The New York Times, where she received an Emmy nomination for outstanding interview, a NPPA award, and a Society of Professional Journalists’ Deadline award for her documentary, Flirting With The Islamic State.

She was also part of the team that received a Public Service Reporting Award from the Scripps Howard Foundation for the Fatal Flaws investigation, which exposed widespread inattention to safety defects by automakers, suppliers and regulators. The investigation contributed to the recall of 60 million vehicles.

Prior to joining The Times, she was a freelance journalist and producer based in New Delhi, covering South Asia for Agence France-Presse. She was also previously a cameraperson for The Associated Press.

Born and raised in Penang, Malaysia, Teng is currently based in Washington, DC.
