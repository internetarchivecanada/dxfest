Biography

Kathleen Lingo is a four-time Emmy Award–winning documentary producer whose work has earned four Oscar nominations, two Peabody Awards, two IDA Awards, and two Cinema Eye Honors. She is currently an independent producer working across a slate of projects, and author of the bestselling Substack Lingo on Docs, covering the evolution of the independent documentary landscape and highlighting the leaders catalyzing a new, exciting era in nonfiction film.

Previously, Lingo served as Editorial Director for Film and Television at The New York Times, where she created and led the production of the paper’s slate of nonfiction feature films and television series grounded in Times journalism. She produced five acclaimed feature films, including the Oscar nominated *Time*, directed by Garrett Bradley, and *Some Kind of Heaven*, directed by Lance Oppenheim, along with nonfiction series such as *The 1619 Project*, reaching wide audiences through outlets including Hulu, HBO, Netflix, and CNN.

Before that, Lingo served as Executive Producer of The New York Times’ Op-Docs, helping launch the series and then leading it as it grew into a defining voice in short-form nonfiction film. During her tenure, Op-Docs published 250 short documentaries, amassing millions of views and premiering films at major festivals including Telluride, Sundance, TIFF, Sheffield, IDFA, and Tribeca. Op-Docs won two IDA Awards under hear leadership for Best Short Form Series: one for the series overall, and another for *A Moment in Mexico*, a series of documentary films by Mexican directors about Mexico.

Kathleen Lingo is commissioning editor of Opinion Video and executive producer of The New York Times’ Op-Doc series. Since joining Op-Docs in 2013, Lingo has worked on more than 150 short, VR and interactive documentaries that have won two Emmy Awards, a Peabody and an Oscar nomination for best short documentary. She is a graduate of the University of Chicago.

From the 2016 catalogue, p. 38

Kathleen Lingo is the coordinating producer of The New York Times’ Op-Doc series. Before joining The Times, she worked on news and documentary projects at HBO, IFC, WNYC, and Jigsaw Productions. Last year, she won an Emmy as part of the team for A Short History of the Highrise produced by The New York Times and the National Film Board of Canada.
