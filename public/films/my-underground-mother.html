About the film

Tamar told her daughter one story about who she was: a fiery redhead, a fighter to the bone who fled her native Poland one step ahead of the Nazis. Marisa Fox grew up with no reason to doubt any of it. Then, twenty years after her mother's death, a conversation with a great-aunt who was dying opens a door that Tamar had kept firmly shut.

What follows is a reporter's work as much as a daughter's: combing archives, following faint trails through records that official histories overlooked, and finding women across continents who had their own reasons to stay silent. Fox braids documents, testimony and on-the-ground reporting, with animation that gives shape to memory, into an account as rigorous as it is tender.

My Underground Mother

recovers a truth that institutions left buried, and asks what we inherit from the stories our parents cannot bring themselves to tell.
