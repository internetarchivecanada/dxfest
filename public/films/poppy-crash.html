Directed by Andalusia Knoll Soloff

About the film

As addiction to the opioid fentanyl surges in the U.S., causing an unprecedented number of overdoses, violence spikes in the mountains of Guerrero, Mexico. Fentanyl is cheaper and more potent, which has slashed natural opium prices, leaving the indigenous poppy farmers in Guerrero with few options for survival. Poppy Crash chronicles the stories of three indigenous farmers who worked in the opium harvest since they were children and suddenly had their livelihood ripped out from under them. Gustavo attempted to migrate to the U.S., and following his deportation, he joined the rank and file of a local crime group and now works as a hitman. Gutierrez substituted poppy with corn, but with a 90% cut in profits, he can’t make ends meet. Yulis, a young mother, traveled thousands of miles from home, leaving her two young children behind to work as a migrant farmworker, but suffered from wage theft. Their dependence on the international drug economy and the volatile market has left these communities with an uncertain future where increased poverty and violence is evident.
