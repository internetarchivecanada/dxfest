Rights from the Ground Up

Friday, November 6, 2026 · 9:30am–10:30am · The Johns Hopkins University Bloomberg Center

Surveillance & privacyMigration & bordersPress freedom & journalists at riskLegal & safety for reporters

The rights journalists exercise — to film in public, to request government records, to refuse to identify sources — belong to every citizen. But unlike professional journalists, whose rights are defended by the in-house counsel, the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, the Committee to Protect Journalists, and a network of other press freedom organizations, ordinary citizens face the consequences of exercising those rights largely alone. As government conduct has grown more aggressive — masked immigration agents, surveillance of protesters, retaliation against public employees who speak out — the gap between legal rights and practical protection has widened dangerously.

A Part II workshop on Saturday led by the Freedom of the Press Foundation covers physical and digital security for non-journalists: how to document encounters with law enforcement, protect communications, and understand what protections actually apply.
