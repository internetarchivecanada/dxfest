Docs in Progress Pitch

Friday, October 11, 2019 · 9:45 AM–12:15 PM · Salon, Eaton Hotel

War, conflict & the militaryHealth & medicineMigration & bordersWomen, gender & sexualityFunding & distributionChina & East AsiaLatin America

Six projects originally presented by documentary organizations Docs In Progress and The D-Word at Peer Pitch programs in four regions of the country have been selected to return to Washington DC for an industry pitch at the Double Exposure Investigative Film Festival fifth addition. The pitching filmmakers present their projects to a panel that includes representatives from across the documentary industry.

PITCH PROJECTS:

Buying Time (Sara Barger – Washington DC)

As a Virginia family faces their 10-year old daughter’s brain cancer, they are doing everything they can do to keep her alive and alleviate her pain, including breaking federal laws by buying cannabis products and having them shipped across state lines.

Dark Summit (Amy Marquis – Boulder, CO)

Mefloquine is an anti-malarial drug with side effects — ranging from anxiety and hallucinations to suicide and homicidal rage — that have affected millions of injured soldiers, diplomats, and civilians. This is a global crisis, but for the filmmaker, it’s also personal: Her life partner was among the first American civilians to take the drug in 1992, and it’s affected every moment of his life since.

The Long Rescue (Jennifer Huang – Oakland, CA)

Hope, Sara, and Carrie come of age in a clandestine shelter for survivors of child sex trafficking in Cebu, Philippines. We follow them over five years as they rebuild their lives and re-enter the world, only to confront the same poverty, predators, and pimps of their childhood.

Norteños (Rodrigo Dorfman and Peter Eversoll – Durham, NC)

After decades of living as immigrants in the United States, several “Norteños” return to their hometown in rural Michoacan, Mexico, bringing a piece of the good old USA back with them. This is an unfiltered and nuanced portrait of the people redefining the boundaries of a new Global South through their experiences in el Norte.

Queer (Cai Thomas – Chicago, IL and Serena Ajbani – Washington DC)

This interactive documentary series is a community-led documentary walking tour outlining the personal and collective queer histories as they relate to place. The stories include an urban planner and choreographer in Los Angeles who first understood his queerness through experiences on public transportation; a Black trans actor who found self-acceptance in her life shaped by community spaces in New Orleans; and a two-spirited person in the Kanien’kéha:ka Territory where Tio:tia’ke/Montreal is located, complicating the notion of queer belonging on stolen land.

Undue Burdens (Andrea Raby – Chicago, IL)

In many midwestern states, TRAP laws force women seeking abortions to travel hundreds of miles to the nearest clinic and face waiting periods, meaning many women struggle to afford the time and expense involved in accessing the procedure. That’s where practical abortion funds like Midwest Access Coalition come in. They are following in the footsteps of organizations like Jane that provided access to abortions prior to Roe v. Wade.
