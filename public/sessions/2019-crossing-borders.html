Pitch · Double Exposure 2019

Crossing Borders

Saturday, October 12, 2019 · 10:00 AM–12:30 PM · Eaton Cinema

Whistleblowers & leaksHealth & medicineMigration & bordersIndigenous peoples & landWomen, gender & sexualityFunding & distribution

A new initiative of Double Exposure 2019, Crossing Borders brings representatives from film programming organizations and journalistic organizations together to give feedback on select projects in front of a live audience. Newsrooms moving print projects into film, and filmmakers venturing into investigative territory, show excerpts of their work and receive feedback from across the aisle. The goal for each side is to raise impact through input from colleagues drawing on unaccustomed expertise. In practice, this means traditionally text-based news operations will hear directly from film programmers about how to strengthen the visual story they are telling. Filmmakers who may lack a background in journalism can learn more about using journalism to undergird character-driven stories and connect them to the broader issues at stake.

Projects being presented:

MARTHA, A film by Anne Alvergue and Debra McClutchy

She was once as famous as Jackie O. And then she tried to take down a President. Martha is a portrait of the unlikeliest of whistleblowers: Martha Mitchell, a Republican cabinet wife who was gas-lighted by the Nixon Administration to keep her quiet. It offers a female gaze on Watergate through the voice of the woman herself.

A BROKEN TRUST, A film by Maren Machles and Carrie Cochran

There’s a place in America where the criminal justice system can legally discriminate. For Indigenous sexual assault survivors on tribal lands, their legal status as an “Indian” sends their case through an entirely different criminal justice system. And in some tribal communities, there’s a common belief here that it’s not if you will be assaulted, but when. This story is a rare glimpse into a severely stressed and complex system and the lives of the people who work within it, trying to make their communities better.

DEEPSIDE A film by Jason Fitzroy Jeffers and Jess Swanson

An independent investigation into the drowning of a Broward county teenager raises questions about the conduct and reporting of the police and medical examiner’s office.
