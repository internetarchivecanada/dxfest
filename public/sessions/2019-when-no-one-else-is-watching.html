When No One Else Is Watching

Thursday, October 10, 2019 · 1:15 PM–2:30 PM · Beverly Snow Room, Eaton Hotel

Documentary craft

Before the public sets eyes on many of the most compelling investigative works, journalists, filmmakers, and even human rights advocates encounter myriad ethical questions along the way. Speakers on this panel are immortalizing people in crisis, sometimes at their most vulnerable: in the immediate aftermath of mass violence (as in Emily Taguchi’s AFTER PARKLAND), or in the throes of acute bodily trauma (such as Luke Lorentzen’s MIDNIGHT FAMILY) or, in the case of the advocacy group Witness, filming victims of human rights abuses in order to further public engagement, policy change, and justice. We’ll unpack the moral issues involved, as filmmakers and advocates grapple with subjects at their most fragile and exposed. What does a filmmaker reveal? What is left off-screen? What is the moral compass that guides their encounters with vulnerable subjects, and how does this shape their storytelling decisions “when no one else is watching,” to cite author and philosopher Aldo Leopold.

With Luke Lorentzen, Palika Makam, Emily Taguchi and Yatziri Tovar. Moderated by Carrie Lozano.
